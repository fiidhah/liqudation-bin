6.4 User Story
"As a customer or buyer, I want to register and log in to the SmartBin Liquidation Bin Store, browse liquidation bins with daily descending price schedules, search and filter certified liquidation merchandise by category, condition, and price, and add items to my cart or wishlist so that I can purchase high-value products at steep discounts. I want to apply promotional discount coupons, complete secure online payments via an integrated payment gateway, receive real-time order confirmation, and track my package through picking, packing, shipping, and delivery milestones, while viewing past orders and itemized invoices. As a store manager, I want to log into the store manager portal, intake liquidation stock manifests, inspect item condition, assign physical bin locations (Bin A–Z), configure and override daily markdown pricing, review the pending customer order queue, pick and pack items from physical bins, update shipment and fulfillment statuses, and view warehouse turnover analytics. As a system administrator, I want to oversee system operations, manage store manager and customer accounts, configure the global 7-day markdown pricing engine, schedule weekend lucky draws and pick winners, monitor real-time order volume and sales revenue, and export comprehensive financial and inventory compliance reports."
6.5 Scenario
A scenario is a detailed description of a particular situation or sequence of events that helps illustrate how a system or process works. In software engineering, scenarios are often used to show how users interact with a system to achieve specific goals. For example, a scenario might describe how a customer logs into a liquidation store portal, browses daily markdown bins, applies discount coupons, and tracks shipment status. Scenarios make it easier to understand user needs, test different conditions, and visualize the flow of operations within a system. They serve as practical examples that bridge the gap between theoretical requirements and real-world usage.
